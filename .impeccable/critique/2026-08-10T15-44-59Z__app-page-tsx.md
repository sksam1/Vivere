## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Does not read as templated AI slop on the whole. No gradient-text misuse beyond the brand-justified gold-sheen, no glassmorphism outside two licensed nav cases, no hero-metric stat blocks, Track A/B cards are genuinely differentiated rather than identical twins, and the I/II/III and 01-04 numerals are real sequences, not decorative eyebrows. Two components do betray generic-template DNA and directly contradict the site's own "zero border-radius, no exceptions" rule: the `rounded-full` step badge in Phases.tsx:61 and the `rounded-full` "Foundation"/"Edge" pills in Tracks.tsx:41,87 — exactly the kind of stock SaaS "how it works"/"pricing tier" shape the brand's own DESIGN.md rejects. Separately, the documented hero display scale (`.text-display`, up to 5.4rem) is dead code — the actual hero H1 tops out at 3.3rem, nearly the same size as ordinary section H2s, flattening the intended monumental-inscription hierarchy.

**Deterministic scan**: `detect.mjs` over `app/` returned 4 advisory-only findings, no hard failures. Three are undocumented colors inside the transactional-email HTML template (`app/api/inquiry/route.ts:107,110,113`) and its `border-radius: 12px` — legitimate, since email HTML can't use the site's CSS tokens and isn't part of the visual system. The fourth is an undocumented `rgba(255,244,214,0.55)` drop-shadow color in `Coin.tsx:55` — a real, if minor, one-off outside the documented gold ramp. No false positives to flag.

**Browser visualization**: not available in this environment (no browser-automation tool connected this session). ## Overall Impression

This is a well-executed, genuinely distinctive brand site — the Roman/Stoic direction is carried all the way through copy, type, motion, and the coin motif, and it mostly avoids the generic-AI tells that plague most single-page brand sites. The gut-reaction gap is that the site's craft is uneven between the "hero" moments (coin flip, ember field, pinned disciplines, masked headlines — all excellent) and the one moment that actually matters commercially: the contact form. That's where the accessibility gaps concentrate (no live-region announcements, no error-to-field linking, identical focus/error color), where the one real usability bug lives (the coin click-to-flip-back conflict, though that's outside the form itself), and where a hardcoded year and a hydration-mismatch risk sit unnoticed. The single biggest opportunity: harden the inquiry form to match the polish of everything above it, since it's the only screen where a visitor's decision converts into revenue.

## What's Working

1. **Accessible heading structure, done for real, not just claimed.** Exactly one `<h1>` in the whole page (Hero.tsx:100), every section headline is an `<h2>`, correctly nested `<h3>`/`<h4>` beneath (Tracks.tsx:53,99; Phases.tsx:64). Verified by reading the tree, not assumed from convention.
2. **Reduced-motion support that actually removes work, not just slows it.** `EmberField.tsx:35` returns before any canvas drawing starts when `prefers-reduced-motion` matches — motes are never drawn, not just stilled. `Disciplines.tsx:39-66` ships a completely separate, clean stacked-list fallback instead of degrading the pinned version in place.
3. **`CoinFlip`'s accessible-name design is genuinely clever**: `aria-label={`${motto}. ${trigger}.`}` (CoinFlip.tsx:34) encodes both coin faces into one static label instead of trying to expose live 3D-flip state to assistive tech, and focus/blur handlers give real keyboard parity with hover, not a hover-only trap.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Contact form gives no accessible confirmation of success or failure at the site's one conversion moment.**
- **Why it matters**: A screen-reader user who submits an inquiry gets no auditory signal it went through, and no indication which field is wrong on validation failure. This is the single highest-stakes interaction on the site and it's currently silent for assistive-tech users.
- **Fix**: Wrap the success/error status swap (Contact.tsx:88-103, 192-196) in an `aria-live="polite"` region. Give each error `<span>` (Contact.tsx:259) an `id`, and reference it via `aria-describedby` on the corresponding input in `Field` (Contact.tsx:249-256).
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden` (contact form error/empty states + accessibility)

**[P1] `stone` (#837a68) fails WCAG AA 4.5:1 against both documented backgrounds.**
- **Why it matters**: Verified by contrast calculation: vs. obsidian (#12100c) = 4.48:1 (fails), vs. panel (#1a1712) = 4.21:1 (fails). PRODUCT.md commits explicitly to the 4.5:1 bar. This token colors the trust microcopy right beside the submit button ("Your email is used only to reply to you," Contact.tsx:197) and the creed's explanatory note (Creed.tsx:29,51) and coach caption (About.tsx:35) — exactly the reassurance copy meant to lower the risk of the ask, undercut by being hard to read.
- **Fix**: Lighten `stone` until it clears 4.5:1 on both obsidian and panel, or restrict its use to large-text contexts only (≥18px / bold ≥14px, which only needs 3:1).
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P1] Focus and error states on form fields are visually identical.**
- **Why it matters**: `Field`'s border logic (Contact.tsx:231-233) resolves error and focus to the exact same solid-gold border, differentiated only by a caption below the field. A keyboard user tabbing through a form with a live validation error can't tell "focused, valid" from "invalid" by the field itself.
- **Fix**: Keep the one-accent-hue rule but add a second signal already licensed elsewhere in the system — a background tint shift to `panel-2`, a small inline glyph, or a dashed vs. solid border for the error state.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P1] `SmoothScroll.tsx` reads `window.matchMedia` synchronously during render, risking a hydration mismatch for the exact users the accessibility floor targets.**
- **Why it matters**: SSR always computes `prefersReduced=false` (no `window` on the server); a reduced-motion user's client render can compute `true` on first paint, producing a React hydration mismatch specifically for reduced-motion users.
- **Fix**: Default to the SSR value and flip it via `useEffect`/`useSyncExternalStore` after mount, matching the pattern already used correctly in `EmberField.tsx` and `Disciplines.tsx`.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable audit`

**[P2] `rounded-full` badges contradict the site's own zero-radius rule and read as generic template scaffolding.**
- **Why it matters**: `Phases.tsx:61` (numbered step circle) and `Tracks.tsx:41,87` ("Foundation"/"Edge" pills) are the exact category-reflex shape the brand's anti-references reject — visually indistinguishable from any stock SaaS "how it works" or pricing-tier component, in a system whose entire identity is "every edge is cut, hard, inscriptional."
- **Fix**: Square them to 0px radius per DESIGN.md, or explicitly justify circularity as a coin motif (a gold ring + halo treatment, consistent with `Coin.tsx`).
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P3] The documented hero display scale is dead code, flattening the intended type hierarchy.**
- **Why it matters**: `Hero.tsx:100` uses a bespoke `clamp(1.9rem,3.9vw,3.3rem)` instead of `.text-display` (`clamp(2.4rem,5.6vw,5.4rem)`, defined in globals.css and DESIGN.md). No section on the page ever reaches the documented hero scale, so the H1 and ordinary H2 section headings render at nearly the same size.
- **Fix**: Apply `.text-display` to the actual hero H1, or if the smaller scale was a deliberate later decision, update DESIGN.md/globals.css to match what's shipped so the two don't drift.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable typeset`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer)**: No active-section indicator anywhere in `Nav.tsx` — nothing tracks scroll position, so Jordan has no persistent "where am I" cue across a long single-page scroll. The 300vh scroll-pinned `Disciplines.tsx` section can read as the page "getting stuck" on a fast scroll-flick, a classic first-visit scroll-jacking complaint.

**Riley (Stress-Tester)**: Clicking (not just hovering) a coin in `CoinFlip.tsx` fights itself: `onMouseEnter` sets `flipped=true` (line 29), then the natural act of clicking the `<button>` immediately toggles it back to `false` via `onClick` (line 33) — mouse users who click to confirm the reveal instead flip it back to the obverse. A fully empty contact form can also be submitted (see P0/Error Prevention above) and is only caught after a full round trip to `/api/inquiry`.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent)**: Compounds P0/P1 above directly — `stone`-colored captions failing contrast, no `aria-describedby`/`aria-live` on form errors and success, and the `SmoothScroll` hydration-mismatch risk landing specifically on reduced-motion users. Also, `About.tsx:24` sets the coach portrait's `alt` to `about.title` ("The man behind the mark") — a section heading, not a description of the photo itself, so Sam hears the wrong thing. On the positive side: Sam with reduced motion set gets `Disciplines.tsx`'s clean linear-list fallback instead of a degraded scroll-jack — a real accommodation, not a token one.

- `Testimonial.tsx:28`'s avatar also uses `rounded-full`, technically outside the zero-radius rule too, though visually conventional enough to be lower priority than the Phases/Tracks badges.
- `Testimonial.tsx` has no `<h2>` — likely fine for a quote block, worth confirming it's intentional given every other section has one.
- Desktop `Nav.tsx:8-14` carries 5 text links plus a separate "Begin" CTA, at the edge of the "≤4 minimal choices" guidance (nav counts are conventionally given more slack than in-page decision points).
- `hero.words` row ("Mindset ◆ Wellness ◆ Habits ◆ Lifestyle ◆ Faith", Hero.tsx:86-97) is ambiguous in function on first read — not quite an eyebrow, not quite navigation, could scan as an unexplained tag list.
- `Coin.tsx:55`'s drop-shadow color (`rgba(255,244,214,0.55)`) is a one-off outside the documented gold ramp (flagged by the detector; likely fine as a highlight-white but worth a deliberate DESIGN.md entry if kept).

## Questions to Consider

- The contact form is the one place where the site's Roman-inscriptional restraint (no color-coding, one accent hue) runs headlong into standard error-UX practice (distinct error color). Is a subtler second signal (texture, glyph, dash pattern) enough, or does this form get a deliberate, licensed exception to the one-accent rule?
- The pinned Disciplines section is a genuine "peak" moment but also the site's biggest control trade-off for first-time visitors moving fast. Worth user-testing whether it reads as intentional pacing or as the page stalling?
- Given faith is "the third coin, not preachy, just true" per your own content.ts comment — does the current balance (one coin out of three, one paragraph in About) feel like the right proportion, or under/over-weighted relative to how you'd want a first-time visitor to receive it?
